## Overview of Construction Staffing

Construction staffing firms recruit across commercial buildings, civil infrastructure, industrial turnarounds, and residential developments. Project timelines move fast, crew sizes ramp up and down by phase, and recruiters often need to fill multiple trades simultaneously.

---

## Common Roles & Specialties

| Category | Representative Roles | Typical Work Environments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structural & Carpentry** | Commercial Carpenters, Concrete Form Builders, Ironworkers, Rodbusters, Millwrights | Commercial high-rise, bridge/civil infrastructure, tilt-up concrete sites |
| **Heavy Equipment & Civil** | Excavator Operators, Bulldozer Operators, Crane Operators, Grader/Paver Operators | Earthwork, highway/roadway construction, utility trenching, site prep |
| **Mechanical, Electrical & Plumbing (MEP)** | Commercial Electricians, Pipefitters, Plumbers, Sheet Metal / HVAC Installers | Hospitals, data centers, manufacturing plants, commercial tenant buildouts |
| **Site Support & Skilled Labor** | Riggers, Scaffold Builders, Skilled Trade Helpers, Material Handlers | Active commercial and industrial job sites |

## Key Preliminary Interview Topics for Construction Candidates

When inviting construction tradespeople to a [VeroSkills Preliminary AI Interview](https://www.veroskills.com/platform/ai-interviews), staffing teams typically organize questions around:
1. **Project Environment Experience**: Commercial new construction vs. industrial plant shutdowns vs. civil/roadway projects.
2. **Tools, Blueprints & Equipment**: Experience reading construction drawings, operating specific machinery or lifts, and working with trade-specific tools.
3. **Safety & Site Readiness**: Experience with job-site safety protocols, PPE practices, and working at heights or in confined spaces.
4. **Logistics & Schedule**: Availability for early-morning start times, overtime, per-diem travel projects, or local commuting radius.
